Three requests, two of which reverse yesterday's pass. Yesterday read the DataSnipper captures and drew the opposite conclusion on both counts; the client has now ruled, and the ruling is what ships.
  **The "Get started" button shrinks.** *"Le bouton commencer est bien trop large, fais en sorte qu'il soit bien plus petit et discret."* Yesterday it became a full-width 50 px block because that is how the reference poses its hero call.   **"Prévisionnel" now shows the desktop panel, exactly.** *"J'aimerais que tu répliques sur mobile exactement le même encadré que je t'envoie"*, with a capture of the **desktop** panel — sidebar, "the deck, live" column and the overhanging deliverable card included. The mockup was already rendered inside a 920 px box by `DesktopScale`, but **Tailwind's breakpoints query the window, not the box**: in 920 px of layout sitting on a 390 px screen, `sm:`, `md:` and `lg:` all answered no, so the mockup composed *wide* with its *phone* fallbacks — sidebar collapsed, live column absent, deliverable card back in the flow. Neither the desktop layout nor the phone one: a third that nobody had drawn. A `wide` flag now supplies the other branch of every folded class (a table of whole literals — Tailwind does not concatenate fragments), and the flag is raised **only where `DesktopScale` is in service**, because it also plants the 24 px shim that reserves the floating card's overhang: unconditional, that shim added 26 px to the desktop home page (17 878 → 17 904, caught by measurement). Below 1024 that window used to be rendered *whole* and shrunk. It is cropped by the column edge again, and what changes per tier is the **scale**, so the framing stays comparable: `readCrop()` returns 0.82 above 1024, 0.62 from 768 to 1024, 0.42 below — **430 px of scene visible at a 390 px screen, against 222 px if the desktop scale had been kept**. Still one single `OraAppScene`: a JS read, not two CSS-hidden blocks. The "Save hours…" card in `UseCasesBento` keeps its whole-scene phone rendering — that one is a full-width card, not a half-column, and cropping it showed a left third cut mid-word.

- **The phone site loses its wall of text: one thing at a time, in a real type hierarchy** (client 2026-08-22: *"prends exemple de DataSnipper (pour leur version mobile) et essaye de faire un site mobile aussi minimaliste et bien fait pour mobile, sans inventer d'autre design à part si il le faut"*). On the desktop that is the page's signature and it fits in two lines. In a 350 px column the same figure becomes a seven-line block at 27 px in which nothing says where the title ends, which is literally the complaint of 2026-08-20 (*"we even have words that are the same in the computer version"*). Those rows now stack below `md` (the rule following: `max-md:border-b` where it was `md:border-r`), and the drawing gains what the split cost it — **the balance-sheet card goes from 0.42 to 0.88 scale**. Rows that really are drawing-beside-drawing keep their columns. **Running text has a 14 px floor on phones.** Same treatment for the footer, whose baseline, link columns and legal line ran at 11.5 / 11 / 9.5 px.

It needs a card wide enough for something to be left: on the desktop, 780 px show the left half. On a phone, **1015 px of scene in a 350 px card left only the left third**, cut mid-word — an accident of framing, and exactly what the client has been refusing since 21/08 (*"they just won't have their design or the whole thing to see at once"*). Below 768 the scene is now rendered **whole**, without `cropScale`. Through `useIsPhone`, not a `md:` class: two frames, not two skins, or the page would mount two `OraAppScene` and two WebGL contexts.

### Fixed
- **The Atlas sky was never actually pinned, and nothing in the DOM said so.** The scene carries Tailwind's `sticky` class, but this file's own `<style>` block is injected *after* Tailwind's sheet: at equal specificity (one class against one class) the later rule wins, so a `position:relative` written in `ATLAS_CSS` silently un-pinned it. The planet scrolled past instead of rising, and the markup looked correct throughout. Caught by reading the scene's *computed* `position` rather than trusting the class list — the rule now sets no `position` at all, and the check is written down beside it. (Second trap the same hour, and the one already on record: a backtick inside that template literal broke the build. The comment says so, in the file.)
- **The "Bilan développé" clip now starts at frame 0 every time you reach it** (client 2026-08-13: "quand l'utilisateur arrive sur Bilan développé, que la vidéo reprenne à zéro depuis le début à chaque fois"). It carried a plain `autoPlay`, so it began at page load and looped for however long the reader took to scroll down: whatever they caught was the middle of a demo already under way. It now runs through `InViewVideo`, whose IntersectionObserver resets `currentTime` on entry and pauses on exit — which also stops a permanent video decode on a page already running two WebGL scenes. Measured: paused at 0 on load, 2.79 s after three seconds on the panel, paused on leaving, **0.34 s** 400 ms after coming back.
- **The particle ring's hover cinch was mute in the whole tabbed section** (client 2026-08-13: "remets l'animation où les particules de l'anneau se resserrent"). `ParticleOrbGL` listened on its host's *direct* parent — fine in the bento grid where the decor is laid flat in the card, but in `ShowcaseCards` the decor is wrapped in a `pointer-events: none` mask layer, and an element that is never a pointer target receives no `pointermove` at all. The listener target is now the first ancestor that actually takes the pointer; in the grid the walk stops on its first step, so nothing there changes. Shader, zone and strength untouched — the effect was never broken, it was simply never told the cursor was there. Verified with real pointer input: the arc gathers under the cursor and the chips flee.
- **Table panel no longer overflows narrow viewports.** A `1fr` grid column keeps `min-width: auto`, so it refuses to shrink below its text; the longest row label pushed the panel past the card and the card past the viewport, clipping the amount column and the card title on mobile. Now `minmax(0,1fr)` plus `min-w-0`, which is what lets `truncate` work at all.
